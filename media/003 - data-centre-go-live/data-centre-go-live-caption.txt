How does a data centre actually go LIVE? ⚡

Finishing construction doesn't make a data centre operational. Before critical IT goes live, every system has to prove itself, alone and together:
1️⃣ Design and BIM/VDC coordination
2️⃣ Construction
3️⃣ Commissioning: factory tests, installation checks, functional tests 🧪
4️⃣ Electrical proven: switchgear, UPS, generators, distribution ⚡
5️⃣ Cooling proven: chillers, pumps, CRAHs/CDUs, controls ❄️
6️⃣ Integrated systems testing: pull the utility power and watch what happens 🚨
7️⃣ Operational readiness, then GO-LIVE ✅

Does the UPS hold? Do the generators start? Does cooling stay up? Do the BMS and EPMS raise the right alarms?

Built is not the same as live. The facility has to perform when it matters.

How does a data centre actually go live?

A data centre isn't operational just because construction is complete. Before critical IT workloads go live, the facility has to prove that its electrical, mechanical, cooling, controls and monitoring systems work, individually and together.

DESIGN → BIM/VDC → CONSTRUCTION → COMMISSIONING → INTEGRATED TESTING → OPERATIONAL READINESS → GO-LIVE

→ Electrical: substations, transformers, switchgear, UPS, generators and critical distribution perform to design intent
→ Cooling: chillers, pumps, CRAHs/CDUs and heat rejection hold the environment under real load
→ Commissioning: factory acceptance, installation verification, pre-functional and functional testing
→ Integrated systems testing: if utility power fails, does the UPS hold, do generators start, does switching follow the sequence, does cooling stay available, do BMS and EPMS report correctly?
→ Operational readiness: as-built data, asset records, commissioning records, monitoring baselines and procedures

Good engineering comes down to one question: can the facility perform when it matters?
