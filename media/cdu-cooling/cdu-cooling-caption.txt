How does a CDU cool a high-density AI data centre? ❄️🖥️

As GPU racks push past what air can handle, liquid takes over, and the Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU) sits at the heart of it:
1️⃣ Cold plates pull heat straight off the GPUs and CPUs
2️⃣ The TCS (technology cooling system) loop carries that heat back to the CDU
3️⃣ A plate heat exchanger transfers it to the facility water system (FWS), and the two loops never mix
4️⃣ N+1 pumps with VFDs adjust flow to hold the supply temperature steady
5️⃣ Side-stream filtration keeps the coolant clean
6️⃣ The facility rejects the heat outside, via dry coolers or cooling towers

CDU capacity alone doesn't define the design. The whole loop has to be engineered. 🔧

Air cooling has limits. AI racks are blowing past them.

Direct-to-chip liquid cooling depends on one critical piece of equipment: the Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU).

→ Cold plates capture heat directly at the processors
→ The technology cooling system (TCS) loop returns warm coolant to the CDU
→ A plate heat exchanger moves that heat into the facility water system (FWS), keeping the two loops fully separated
→ N+1 pumps and variable-speed drives hold supply temperature and flow steady as GPU load swings
→ Filtration, pressure control and monitoring protect the cold plates
→ The facility rejects the heat outside

Choosing a CDU by megawatts is only the start. Approach temperature, redundancy, water chemistry and controls all decide whether the system holds up under real AI load.
